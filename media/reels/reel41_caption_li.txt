"You're $40 away from free shipping" quietly nudges bigger carts — shoppers hate paying for delivery, so they add another item.

It is a simple win, and it is built into Ultra.

The mechanic works because it reframes the decision. Instead of asking "do I want to spend more", the shopper sees a gap to close, and closing a gap feels like a win rather than a spend.

Threshold: the $40 figure sits just above typical cart value, close enough to feel reachable, far enough to require adding something.

Framing: "you're $40 away" is phrased as progress, not as a fee avoided — progress is what moves people to act.

Placement: the nudge lives in the cart itself, at the exact moment the shopper is deciding whether the order is finished.

Outcome: bigger average order value, without a discount code, without a sale, without touching margin on the items already in the cart.

This is the kind of detail that separates a theme that merely displays products from one that actively shapes buying behaviour. Ultra is built around small mechanics like this one, working quietly in the background of every cart.

None of it requires the merchant to configure complicated rules. The logic is already part of the storefront, ready to run on day one.
