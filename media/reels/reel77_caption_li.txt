An accessible store is more buyers — readable text, good contrast, buttons that work for everyone. The difference is stopping the loss of customers others ignore.

Ultra is built with that in mind. Accessible design is not a compliance checkbox. It is a conversion tool.

Readable text: clear typefaces, sufficient size, proper line height and spacing. A buyer who cannot read the offer cannot buy it.

Contrast: text and background colours meet WCAG standards. Colour alone never carries information — links, buttons and form states work for colourblind shoppers.

Navigation: keyboard users can reach every element. Screen reader users get semantic structure and descriptive labels. Mobile users get touch targets large enough to hit without frustration.

Buttons: focus states visible, error messages clear, form validation helpful instead of hostile. The checkout does not punish the customer for being different.

The business case is straightforward. The World Health Organization estimates over a billion people live with some form of disability — and many more experience temporary barriers: aging eyesight, a broken arm, a noisy train. An accessible store serves all of them. It also ranks better, loads faster, and feels more trustworthy to everyone.

Ultra handles this in the theme layer, so the store works for all shoppers from day one.
