People rarely buy the first thing they tap. A recently-viewed row lets them jump back instead of hunting. Less friction, more sales.

This is a core conversion principle, and it is built into Ultra, UTD's Shopify theme for high-volume stores.

The mechanism is simple but effective. A customer browses a category, taps a product, reads the description, changes their mind. In most stores, they start the hunt again from scratch — scrolling, filtering, searching. Every extra step is a chance to leave.

Recently-viewed: the row sits persistent on the product page, showing the last products the customer touched. One tap brings them back. No friction, no hunting.

The result compounds across a session. A customer might cycle through ten products before committing. With recently-viewed, that cycle stays fast and stays frictionless. Without it, each loop includes the cost of re-navigation — and that cost adds up.

Friction is the silent killer of conversion. Baymard Institute research on e-commerce UX shows that customers cite "too many steps" and "confusing navigation" as top reasons for cart abandonment. Recently-viewed solves both by keeping the customer's own browsing history one tap away.

Ultra includes this as standard, along with the other small, powerful features that separate themes built for selling from themes built for showing.
