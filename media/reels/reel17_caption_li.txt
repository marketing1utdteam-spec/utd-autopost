Forcing shoppers to create an account kills carts: 26% quit right there, according to Baymard.

That is not a small leak. It is a checkout built to reward loyalty from people who have not bought anything yet, and it charges that price on every single visitor.

Account requirement: an extra form, a password to remember, a reason to close the tab before payment even starts.

Guest checkout: removes the barrier entirely. The shopper pays first, decides on a relationship with the store later.

Express pay: one tap replaces a card form, and the sale closes before hesitation has time to set in.

The logic is simple. Checkout is not the place to negotiate loyalty. It is the place to get out of the way. A store that insists on an account at the exact moment someone is ready to pay is optimizing for the wrong outcome — and Baymard's number shows what that costs in real abandoned carts, not theory.

Allure is built around that principle: guest checkout and express pay in front, account creation pushed to after the sale, where it belongs. The goal is not to collect signups. It is to collect payments.
