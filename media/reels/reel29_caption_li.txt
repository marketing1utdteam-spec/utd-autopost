That empty space is not wasted — it is on purpose.

Whitespace tells the eye something before a single word is read: confident, premium, deliberate. A layout that leaves room to breathe signals a brand that does not need to shout.

Cramming every pixel does the opposite. It signals urgency, clearance, discount bin. The eye reads density as cheap even when the product is not.

Whitespace: the space around a product photo is doing work. It frames the item the way a gallery frames a painting, giving it the visual authority to be looked at rather than scrolled past.

Focus: with fewer competing elements on screen, attention has only one place to land. The product becomes the hero of the page instead of one voice in a crowd of banners, badges and buttons.

Price: this is where it compounds. A price sitting in a calm, uncluttered layout reads as considered. The same number crammed next to five other offers reads as negotiable. Room to breathe makes the price easier to say yes to.

This is the thinking behind the Allure theme. It is built around restraint rather than density, trusting that the product and the price can do their own talking when the layout stops competing with them.
