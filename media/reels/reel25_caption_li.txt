Every extra checkout field is a tiny reason to quit — name, company, address line two, phone.

They add up fast. Each additional field is another moment where a buyer can pause, hesitate, or simply close the tab before finishing.

Fields: name, company, address line two, phone — none of them essential to completing a purchase, all of them capable of ending one.

Approach: ask for the minimum required to process the order. Nothing more, nothing that can wait until after the sale.

Speed: let express pay handle the rest. One tap replaces a form, and the buyer never has to think about typing anything at all.

Result: fewer fields, more finished orders. Checkout stops feeling like a test and starts feeling like a formality.

Ultra keeps checkout tight. Every field on the page has to justify its place, and most fields on most stores do not.

The buyer who reaches checkout has already decided to buy. The only job left for the store is to get out of the way — not add another line to fill in, another label to read, another reason to reconsider.

Ultra is built around that single idea: strip checkout down to what actually matters, and let express pay absorb the friction that used to sit in a dozen scattered fields.
