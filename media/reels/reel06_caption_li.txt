A real store on the Allure theme was losing mobile shoppers. Now everything works one-handed.

The store had been built like a desktop catalogue that happened to load on phones. Buttons sat just out of thumb reach, product pages demanded pinching and zooming, and checkout asked for more taps than a mobile shopper was willing to give before giving up.

Moving to Allure changed the architecture rather than the decoration. Layouts, spacing and controls were rebuilt around a single hand holding a single phone, which is how most visitors actually arrive at a storefront now.

Checkout: mobile checkouts +34%.

Bounce: bounce −19%.

Those two numbers describe the same shift from two directions. Fewer people leave before they engage, and more of the people who do engage complete the purchase. That is what a mobile-first theme is supposed to produce — not a redesign for its own sake, but fewer points where a thumb has to work harder than it should.

Support set it up with the store owner directly, which matters as much as the theme itself. A theme can be phone-first on paper and still be misconfigured in ways that quietly cost checkouts — variant settings, cart drawer behaviour, sticky add-to-cart placement. Getting those right during setup is often the difference between a theme that looks mobile-ready and one that actually converts on mobile.
