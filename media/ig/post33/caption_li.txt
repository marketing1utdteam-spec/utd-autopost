Two stores, same products, same prices. One converts twice as well. Can you guess which — and why?

Store A: big photos, buy button in reach, reviews on the page, ~1s load.

Store B: small images, buy button below the fold, no reviews, ~4s load.

Winner: A, by a lot.

Decision point: 87% of shoppers decide on the product page (Salsify).

Speed: 53% leave a slow one (Google).

Layout and speed alone can double conversion — the same products, the same prices, a completely different outcome.

The difference is not the merchandise. It is whether the storefront is built to answer buying questions before hesitation sets in: photos large enough to trust, a buy button within reach, reviews visible without scrolling, and a load time fast enough that nobody leaves before the page finishes appearing.

That is the gap between a store that merely displays products and one engineered to convert the traffic it already has. More visitors will not fix a slow page or a buried buy button. A better-built storefront will.

The stores that win are not the ones with more traffic. They are the ones built so that the traffic they already have converts.
