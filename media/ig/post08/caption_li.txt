Most store owners check their site the wrong way: they look at it like an owner, not like a first-time visitor with a full inbox and eight open tabs.

The store has about three seconds to answer one question: can I trust this place with my card?

Seven signs, each checkable in under a minute.
Speed: load speed on a real phone.
Message: one clear message above the fold.
Photos: product photos that survive zoom.
Shipping: visible shipping costs before checkout.
Search: working search.
Reviews: reviews where people actually look.
Checkout: no forced registration.

The research behind each sign is public. Google found 53% of mobile visitors leave a page that takes over 3 seconds to load. Baymard Institute's checkout audits show 48% of shoppers abandon carts over unexpected extra costs, and 26% quit because the site wanted an account first. PwC surveyed 15,000 consumers: 32% walk away from a brand they love after a single bad experience.

ASOS is the classic case study. Replacing forced registration with guest checkout cut abandonment by half. On the other side, Baymard estimates $260 billion in US and EU orders are recoverable through better checkout design alone.

The money is not in more traffic. It is in the leaks.

Ten honest minutes and this list find the leak faster than an agency audit. A theme that fails three or more signs is the problem, not the traffic.
