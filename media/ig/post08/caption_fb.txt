Most store owners check their site the wrong way: they look at it like an owner, not like a first-time visitor with a full inbox and eight open tabs. Your store has about three seconds to answer one question: "can I trust this place with my card?"

This carousel is a self-test. Seven signs, each checkable in under a minute: load speed on a real phone, one clear message above the fold, product photos that survive zoom, visible shipping costs before checkout, working search, reviews where people actually look, and a checkout with no forced registration. Count your score honestly.

The research behind each sign is public. Google found 53% of mobile visitors leave a page that takes over 3 seconds to load. Baymard Institute's checkout audits show 48% of shoppers abandon carts over unexpected extra costs, and 26% quit because the site wanted an account first. PwC surveyed 15,000 consumers: 32% walk away from a brand they love after a single bad experience.

ASOS became the classic case study here: when they replaced forced registration with guest checkout, abandonment dropped by half — one change, measurable millions. On the other side, Baymard estimates $260 billion in US and EU orders are recoverable through better checkout design alone. The money isn't in more traffic; it's in the leaks.
