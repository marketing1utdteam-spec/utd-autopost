Choosing a theme looks like a design decision, but it's really an operations decision: it decides a store's speed, its mobile experience, its feature bill and its support contract for years ahead. This checklist is the filter applied before any theme earns a place under a client's brand.

The seven boxes, in order of expense when missed:

Speed: load in under 3 seconds — speed is a theme property, not an app added later.

Mobile: look premium on a phone first, since most buyers never see the desktop version.

Built-in selling: quick view, size charts, mega menu and bundles included, or they get rented monthly as apps.

Checkout access: "Buy" stays one tap away with a sticky add-to-cart and slide-out cart.

Proof: reviews, ratings, stock counters and payment icons shown everywhere.

Editing: full control in the visual editor, with no developer needed.

Support: updates and support that in practice mean the official Shopify Theme Store, where every theme is reviewed and maintained.

Each box has a number behind it.

Speed: Google reports 53% of mobile visits abandon past 3 seconds.

Abandonment: Baymard Institute puts average cart abandonment around 70%, with complicated checkout among the top reasons — and its checkout-usability research estimates large stores can lift conversions by roughly a third through better checkout design alone.

Trust: Stanford's Web Credibility studies found about 75% of users judge a company's credibility by its website design.

The rule after years of theme work: never pay for a theme untested against this list, on a phone, in the free trial, with real products loaded. Every UTD theme is built to pass all seven — the standard the official store's review process holds it to, with an unlimited trial to verify it before spending anything.
