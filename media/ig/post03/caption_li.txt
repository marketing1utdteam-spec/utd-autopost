"The theme is free" is the most convincing lie in eCommerce, mostly because nobody is technically lying: the download really does cost zero.

The bill just arrives later, itemized differently.

Free themes ship deliberately bare. Each missing feature returns as an app subscription.

Reviews: ~$15/mo.
Upsells: ~$20/mo.
Mega menu: ~$9/mo.
Bundles: ~$12/mo.

That is $56 a month, $672+ in year one, forever — for features a good paid theme includes in a single $100 payment. And every bolted-on app injects its own scripts into the storefront, which is precisely how fast stores become slow.

Slow costs more than the subscriptions.

Speed threshold: Google's mobile research found 53% of visitors abandon a page that takes longer than 3 seconds.
Conversion: Akamai measured a 7% conversion drop from just 100 milliseconds of extra delay.
Upside: Deloitte's "Milliseconds Make Millions" study showed a 0.1-second improvement lifts retail conversions by 8.4%.

The reverse experiment has been run publicly. Swappie rebuilt its mobile store for speed and documented a 42% increase in mobile revenue in web.dev's case study. Vodafone's speed optimization delivered +8% in sales in a controlled A/B test. None of that came from new marketing — just from removing the weight the cheap setup had accumulated.

Free themes are a fine way to practice, and an expensive way to do business. If a store is meant to make money, the $100 theme with everything built in is the cheapest thing to buy for it.
