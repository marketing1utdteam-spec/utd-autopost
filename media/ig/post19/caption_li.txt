Furniture and décor might be the hardest honest category in ecommerce: high prices, bulky shipping, and a product whose entire value is how it feels in a room — sold through a six-inch screen.

Generic themes treat a sofa like a t-shirt: thumbnail, price, button. Here is what the niche actually requires.

Rooms, not thumbnails: décor is sold by atmosphere, full-width room scenes instead of product-on-white grids.

Shop-the-look: one room photo where every item is tappable, the highest-intent purchase path in the niche.

Materials up close: zoomable texture that replaces the showroom hand-touch.

Delivery answered early: bulky-item shipping anxiety kills orders at checkout unless costs and timelines are visible upfront.

Reviews with photos: real customers' interiors are the strongest proof a décor store can show.

Article, the Canadian DTC furniture brand, built a nine-figure business on exactly these mechanics: oversized room photography, transparent delivery pricing displayed before checkout, and customer-photo galleries woven into product pages. The company became one of North America's fastest-growing furniture retailers before its acquisition, outconverting legacy players with 10× their catalog. The container did the differentiating; the furniture was comparable.

In décor, the theme is not presentation. It is the substitute for the showroom. Maison, a Gain preset in the official Shopify Theme Store, ships the room-scene sections, shop-the-look and zoomable galleries natively.
