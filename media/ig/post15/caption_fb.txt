Every week someone asks us the same question: "Shopify, WooCommerce, or something custom?" It sounds like a technology decision. It isn't. It's a decision about what you want to spend your next three years thinking about — your products, or your infrastructure.

Here's what the platform actually takes off your plate. Hosting and uptime: Shopify's infrastructure absorbs Black Friday traffic spikes that crash self-hosted stores every November. Payments: Shop Pay, Apple Pay, Google Pay and 3D-Secure compliance ship built-in — no gateway integrations, no PCI audits on your side. Security: SSL, patches, DDoS protection are the platform's problem, handled by a security team larger than most companies' entire staff. And scale: the same store that takes your first order can take your millionth without re-platforming.

The numbers behind the choice: Shopify powers over 4 million live stores and processed $292+ billion in merchandise volume in 2024 — roughly 10% of US ecommerce. Its uptime SLA track record runs at 99.99%. During Black Friday–Cyber Monday 2024 the platform handled peaks of 80+ million requests per minute without going down — a load test no store owner could ever afford to run alone.

Gymshark is the case that settles the argument. Ben Francis's fitness brand originally ran on Magento, self-managed. On Black Friday 2015 — the most important sales day of the year — the site crashed and stayed down for hours; Francis has publicly estimated the outage cost them around £100,000 in lost sales and, worse, the trust of customers who'd waited all year. The company migrated to Shopify, and the next Black Friday ran without a hiccup. Gymshark scaled past $500M in revenue on the platform and became its flagship enterprise story. The lesson wasn't that Magento is bad — it's that infrastructure is a full-time job, and it wasn't the job Gymshark wanted.
