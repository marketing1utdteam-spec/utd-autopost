The scariest parts of running a store are the ones you never think about — until they break.

On a self-hosted store, the infrastructure work falls entirely on the merchant.

Hosting: staying up during a traffic spike, without warning.

Security: payment security and PCI compliance, SSL, patches.

Protection: fraud and DDoS protection, monitored around the clock.

Taken together, that is not a checklist. It is a full-time job, one most merchants never signed up for and cannot staff.

On Shopify, that job belongs to the platform. It is handled by a security team larger than most companies, running infrastructure built for scale rather than bolted on after the fact.

The platform processed hundreds of billions in sales last year and held through Black Friday peaks no single store could load-test on its own. That is not a marketing claim, it is the baseline the platform is built to survive every year.

Once hosting, security and compliance stop being the merchant's problem, attention moves to where it actually pays off: the storefront. A great theme earns its keep there — in how the store looks, loads and converts, not in whether the servers stay up.

Infrastructure is invisible when it works. It is the whole business when it fails.
