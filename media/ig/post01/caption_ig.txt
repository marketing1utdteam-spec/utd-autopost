Selling through Instagram DMs feels natural until it doesn't.

That moment arrives around your tenth order in one week. The problem isn't effort—it's architecture. A DM sale depends entirely on you: your response speed, your working hours, your ability to chase payment without sounding desperate. There's no product page answering questions while you sleep, no secure checkout building buyer confidence, no abandoned cart to recover.

Baymard Institute research puts average cart abandonment at about 70%—and that's on real stores with email recovery. In a DM flow, there is no cart and no recovery. The sale dies in "seen". Worse: careful buyers won't send card details in chat, so your most qualified customers are the ones who walk away.

Gymshark proves the shift works. Ben Francis started in 2012 selling through social and word of mouth. The turning point was moving to a proper Shopify storefront—which let the brand take orders around the clock and scale into one of the biggest fitness labels in the world, valued at over a billion pounds.
