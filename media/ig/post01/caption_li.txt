Selling through Instagram DMs feels natural at first: someone asks "is this available?", you answer, you get paid, everyone's happy. It works right up until the moment it doesn't — and that moment usually arrives with the tenth order in one week.

The problem is not effort, it is architecture. A DM sale depends on one person: response speed, working hours, the ability to chase a payment without sounding desperate. There is no product page answering questions while you sleep, no secure checkout making the buyer feel safe, no cart that waits patiently.

The research explains what that hesitation costs.
Abandonment: Baymard Institute's long-running study puts average cart abandonment at about 70% — and that is on real stores, where an abandoned cart can still be recovered with an email.
Recovery: in a DM flow there is no cart and no recovery. The sale simply dies in "seen".
Trust: buyers are routinely warned never to send card details in chats, so the most careful customers are exactly the ones who walk away.

Gymshark is the classic case. Ben Francis started in 2012 selling gym wear through social channels and word of mouth. The audience came from social; the sales machine was the store.
