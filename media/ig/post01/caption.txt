Selling through Instagram DMs feels natural at first: someone asks "is this available?", you answer, you get paid, everyone's happy. It works right up until the moment it doesn't — and that moment usually arrives with the tenth order in one week.

The problem isn't effort, it's architecture. A DM sale depends on you personally: your response speed, your working hours, your ability to chase a payment without sounding desperate. There is no product page answering questions while you sleep, no secure checkout making the buyer feel safe, no cart that waits patiently. Every hesitation ends the deal, because asking twice in a chat feels awkward.

The research explains what that hesitation costs. Baymard Institute's long-running study puts average cart abandonment at about 70% — and that's on real stores, where an abandoned cart can still be recovered with an email. In a DM flow there is no cart and no recovery: the sale simply dies in "seen". Add the trust factor: buyers are routinely warned never to send card details in chats, so your most careful customers are exactly the ones who walk away.

Gymshark is the classic case. Ben Francis started in 2012 selling gym wear through social channels and word of mouth — and the turning point was moving the whole operation onto a proper Shopify storefront, which let the brand take orders around the clock and scale into one of the biggest fitness labels in the world, valued at over a billion pounds. The audience came from social; the sales machine was the store.

Our take after building storefronts for years: DMs are a brilliant marketing channel and a terrible checkout. Keep the conversations — move the transactions.
