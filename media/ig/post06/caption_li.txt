Before a shopper reads a single word on your site, they have already priced your brand. It happens in a blink, it is not rational, and it decides whether typing a card number here feels safe or slightly reckless.

"Expensive" is a learned visual language, and it is remarkably consistent across industries: confident editorial typography instead of default fonts, photography with intention, generous whitespace, small details that behave properly on every screen. None of it requires expensive products. It requires an expensive-looking container. That is literally what a premium theme is — the visual cues of a brand that invested, available off the shelf.

The research is old, famous and still undefeated.
Trust: Stanford's Web Credibility project found that roughly 75% of consumers judge an organization's credibility by its website design alone.
Speed: Gitte Lindgaard's published experiments showed visual first impressions form in about 50 milliseconds, before any text is consciously read.
Conversion: Baymard's checkout studies add the commercial punchline — perceived design quality directly feeds willingness to enter payment details.

Glossier is the textbook case of design doing the heavy lifting: a beauty brand whose calm, editorial, deliberately "expensive-simple" website became part of the product itself, letting a startup sell alongside giants at premium prices.
