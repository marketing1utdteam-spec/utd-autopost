In a physical shop, your best salesperson answers questions, handles doubts, and closes. Online, all of that lands on one page: the product page.

Every ad click, every profile visit, every DM eventually funnels there — and most stores treat it as an afterthought.

A product page that sells does five jobs.
Visuals: shows the product from every angle, photos and video.
Answers: covers size, material and shipping questions before they're asked.
Trust: shows social proof next to the buy button.
Upsell: offers a bigger or complementary option.
Speed: loads before doubt kicks in.

The data says product content is the sale. Salsify's consumer report found 87% of shoppers call product content extremely or very important to the buying decision, and a third have abandoned a purchase because information was missing or wrong. Baymard's audits show images and descriptions are among the top reasons users leave product pages. Deloitte measured that a 0.1s speed improvement lifts retail conversions by 8.4%.

Bellroy is the reference here. Obsessive photography, videos of wallets filling up, comparison sliders — their product pages became a design-school example, and the brand grew into a global leader in a commodity category largely on the strength of those pages. Same product everyone sells; radically better page.

Before another ad is bought, that budget's worth of attention belongs on the page the ad lands on. A $100 theme with a properly built product page outperforms a $1,000/month traffic bill pointed at a weak one.
