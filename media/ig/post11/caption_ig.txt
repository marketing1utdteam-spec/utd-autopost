"More traffic" is usually the wrong diagnosis for slow sales.

If your store converts 1% of visitors, doubling traffic doubles your ad spend. Moving conversion to 2% doubles revenue with the traffic you already have. The cheapest lever is the storefront itself.

When a store swaps a generic theme for a purpose-built one—same products, same prices, same traffic—measurable things change: faster load time, a hero that states the offer, collection filters that match how people shop, product pages with proof next to the button, checkout with fewer steps.

The documented numbers: Google/SOASTA modeling shows bounce probability jumps 32% as load time goes 1s→3s. Walmart measured up to +2% conversions for every 1 second of speed. Deloitte: +8.4% retail conversions per 0.1s. Baymard: better checkout design alone could recover $260B in abandoned orders across the US and EU. None of these require a new visitor.

COOK, the UK frozen-meals retailer: a design overhaul lifted conversions by 7% and cut bounce by 7 points. AliExpress cut load time ~36% and saw 10.5% more orders.

Speed and layout are not cosmetics. They're revenue mechanics.

Before buying traffic, fix the container it lands in. A theme swap is a weekend project with a measurable payoff.
