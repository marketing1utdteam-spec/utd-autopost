"We need more traffic" is the default diagnosis for slow sales. It is usually wrong.

If a store converts 1% of visitors, doubling traffic doubles cost. Moving conversion to 2% doubles revenue with the traffic already there. The cheapest lever is the storefront itself.

Here is what actually changes when a generic theme is swapped for a purpose-built one — same products, same prices, same traffic.
Speed: faster first paint.
Offer: a hero that states it plainly.
Browsing: collection pages with filters that match how people shop.
Proof: product pages with evidence next to the button.
Checkout: fewer steps in the path.

The public numbers behind each lever.
Bounce: Google/SOASTA modeling shows bounce probability jumps 32% as load time goes 1s to 3s.
Speed: Walmart measured up to +2% conversions for every 1 second of speed improvement.
Conversions: Deloitte found +8.4% retail conversions per 0.1s.
Checkout: Baymard estimates better checkout design alone could recover $260B in abandoned orders across the US and EU.

None of this requires a single new visitor.

COOK, the UK frozen-meals retailer, is a documented case: a site performance and design overhaul lifted conversions by 7% and cut bounce by 7 points. AliExpress cut load time ~36% and saw 10.5% more orders.

Speed and layout are not cosmetics. They are revenue mechanics, and a theme swap is the cheapest way to move them.
