No analytics dashboard has a metric called "theme problem." That's what makes a cheap theme the most expensive line item you'll never see: it doesn't fail loudly, it bleeds quietly — through four channels that all show up in your reports disguised as something else.

The four bleeds on the banner: speed (cheap code is heavy code, and every 100ms costs up to 7% of conversions); bounce (as load creeps from 1 to 3 seconds, bounce probability jumps 32% — recorded in analytics as "weak traffic," blamed on ads); trust (75% of shoppers judge credibility by design, so a template look silently caps the prices you can charge); and the app tax ($672+/year renting features the theme should have shipped, each app adding more scripts to the pile — feeding bleed number one).

The reverse experiment proves the direction. When Vodafone's ecommerce team improved just the loading metric (31% faster LCP) in a controlled A/B test — same products, same prices, same traffic — sales rose 8%. When COOK redesigned for performance, conversions rose 7% and bounce fell 7 points. Nothing about the products changed in either case; the bleed simply stopped. That's the quiet money a cheap theme costs — visible only when someone finally plugs the leak.

My take: audit the container before you audit the campaigns. If your store is on a bloated template, every ad dollar you spend is working at a discount.
