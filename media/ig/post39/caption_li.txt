Shopify's free theme is a fine start — here's the honest table of what $100 changes.

The comparison comes down to five areas, and the differences compound fast once a store starts scaling.

Speed: lean, optimised code versus heavy defaults that were never built for a specific niche.

Features: quick view, swatches, mega-menu and upsells built in from day one — or rented separately as apps for $600+/yr, each one adding its own load time and its own bill.

Mobile: designed phone-first, versus a desktop layout that has simply been scaled down to fit a smaller screen.

Look: a premium, distinctive design versus a template thousands of other stores are already running, which makes differentiation harder the moment a shopper has seen it elsewhere.

Support: real help and free updates versus a forum thread that may or may not get answered.

None of this is about whether the free theme works. It does. The question is what it costs once quick view, swatches, a mega-menu and upsells are added back in one app at a time, each with its own monthly fee stacking on top of the last.

$100 once, versus a recurring app bill and a slower store. The math is not close, and it only gets less close the longer a store runs on rented features instead of built-in ones.
